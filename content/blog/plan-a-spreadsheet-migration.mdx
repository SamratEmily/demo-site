A spreadsheet often holds several jobs at once. A stock sheet might track purchases, double as a delivery checklist and supply the figures for a monthly report. Before you migrate it, find out which of those jobs people actually depend on.

## Map the work around the sheet

Ask the people who use the spreadsheet to walk through one real transaction. Note who enters it, who checks it and which report depends on it. Follow one exception as well, such as a returned item or a payment that covers two invoices. Exceptions reveal rules that may live in a comment, a colour or someone's memory.

## Decide what to import

Choose which records the new app needs to do its job. Open orders and unpaid invoices usually need enough detail for staff to act on them. Older records may only need to stay available in a read-only archive. Give each customer, product or order a stable identifier so that matching names do not merge unrelated records.

## Rehearse with a copy

Import a copy without interrupting daily work. Compare record counts, balances and a few individual records with the source sheet. Ask staff to complete normal tasks in the test app. If an order cannot be matched or a date is ambiguous, keep a list for someone familiar with the data to resolve.

## Agree when the new app takes over

Set a cutoff, assign someone to reconcile the final import and tell everyone where to record new work. Keep the original sheet read-only so staff can refer back to it without maintaining two copies. Review the first reporting cycle with the people who used to prepare it.
