Most businesses reach a point where notebooks and spreadsheets stop working. The next question is usually: do we buy a ready-made product, or have software built for us? Both can be the right answer. These questions help you decide.

## Start with the problem, not the software

Write down, in plain words, what goes wrong today. For example: "we miss installment payments because nobody has a list of what is due", or "we don't know our stock until we count it". A clear list of problems is the most useful thing you can bring to any software company, and it makes it easier to compare options.

## Five questions to ask

### Does a product already do most of what you need?

If a ready-made product covers most of your list, start there. It is usually faster to set up and costs less up front. Ask for a demo with your own examples, not the vendor's.

### How different is your way of working?

If your business has rules that most products don't support, such as profit shared with investors or a special approval step, you will spend a lot of time working around a product. That is a sign that custom software, or a product that can be adjusted, will serve you better.

### Who will use it, and in which language?

Software your staff find hard to use won't be used. Check that the screens make sense to the people who will enter the data every day, and that it works in the language they are comfortable with.

### What happens when you need a change?

Ask how changes are requested, how long they usually take and what they cost. With custom software, make sure you receive the source code and enough documentation for another developer to continue the work.

### What does it cost over three years?

Compare the total, not only the first payment: licence or subscription fees, hosting, support and the cost of changes. A cheaper start can become more expensive over time, and the other way round.

## A middle path

Many businesses start with a ready-made product that can be adjusted, then add custom features as they grow. Our own products, such as [Gari Becho](/en/products/gari-becho/), are built this way.
